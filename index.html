The story behind the name

Cero means zero. That is the whole design brief.

Omni Cero — from Latin omni ("all") and Spanish cero ("zero") — is an intraoperative imaging and surgical navigation software suite for Windows. Installed as Live C-arm + Cero or XVR + Cero Integrated, it brings the whole imaging room into a single offline workstation: live C-arm capture, preoperative CT/MRI review, DICOM archiving, and 3D XVR surgical navigation.

It started with an observation from the imaging room itself. The C-arm already produces beautiful real-time images, and the preoperative CT already holds the 3D truth — yet the two live in separate worlds. Technologists shuttle between a capture box, a viewer and a navigation cart while the clock on the sterile field keeps running. Omni Cero was built to close that loop in software: capture, reconstruct, register, navigate — without a single cable leaving the room.

The name is the promise. We hold every release to the number cero: zero cloud dependency, zero telemetry, zero patient data leaving the hospital, and zero dropped frames on a 30 FPS stream. Omni because one workstation should cover every modality in the room. When a surgeon asks "where is the tip?", the answer should come from the images already being taken — not from a server farm.

The "cero" checklist

  • Zero cloud — every computation runs on the workstation in the room
  • Zero telemetry — no phone-home, no analytics, ever
  • Zero dropped frames — 30 FPS fluoroscopy buffered locally, frame by frame
  • Zero data egress — DICOM studies never leave your hospital LAN

FAQ

Omni Cero, XVR and the rest — answered

What the workstation does, what XVR technology means, and how licensing works inside the operating room.

What is Omni Cero?

Omni Cero is an offline Windows software workstation for intraoperative imaging and surgical navigation, shipped as two editions. Live C-arm + Cero provides the Live C-Arm Video Station for 30 FPS fluoroscopy capture plus an Intraoperative PACS with 3D multi-planar reconstruction. XVR + Cero Integrated combines the Cero suite with 3D XVR Surgical Navigation — X-ray Volume Registration for guidance without tracking hardware. Everything computes on the machine in the room — nothing is sent to the cloud.

What does XVR mean?

XVR stands for X-ray Volume Registration. It is a neural 2D/3D registration technique that aligns each live fluoroscopy frame from the C-arm with the patient's preoperative CT or MRI volume. Instead of optical trackers or fiducial arrays, the XVR pose solver estimates the 6-DOF relationship between the X-ray image and the 3D anatomy in real time — giving sub-millimeter surgical navigation from the C-arm you already own.

Does Omni Cero need an internet connection?

No. Every application runs fully offline — no cloud services, no telemetry, no activation servers. DICOM studies move only across your hospital LAN, and licenses are Ed25519-signed files verified mathematically on the device itself.

Which C-arms and capture cards are supported?

Omni Cero works with C-arms from Siemens Healthineers, GE Healthcare, Philips and Ziehm Imaging through any USB UVC capture card with HDMI, DVI, BNC or RCA input. Capture is driver-free on Windows 10/11 and buffers directly at 30 FPS.

What are the system requirements?

Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), 8 GB RAM minimum — 16 GB recommended for 3D CT volumes — and 2.5 GB of free disk space. No Python, Java or CUDA runtime is required.

How is Omni Cero licensed?

Licenses are issued by Omni IDE and hardware-bound to the motherboard, CPU and system UUID, so they cannot be copied to another machine. Each license is Ed25519-signed and verified on-device — no phone-home is needed to keep working. Download an installer from the download section to get started.
